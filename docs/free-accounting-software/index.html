Free Accounting Software You Install and Own

Most software marketed as "free accounting software" is free for 30 days, free up to 20 invoices, or free only while you stay on a cloud plan that reads every transaction in your ledger. Independent Books is free in the software-freedom sense: MIT licensed, no seat count, no company limit, no expiry, and no account to create.

It is a desktop application. You download an 8.3 MB installer, it starts a server bound to 127.0.0.1, and it writes your books to a single SQLite file on your own disk. Nothing is uploaded, because there is nowhere for it to be uploaded to.

What "free" means here

The MIT licence lets you use the software commercially, modify it, and redistribute it. There is no open-core split where reports or multi-company support sit behind a paywall, and there is no licence server that can switch your ledger off.

The practical consequence is that the cost of your accounting records stops being a recurring expense. A five-year run of a typical Indian SMB plan costs more than a laptop; a five-year run of this costs nothing after download.

  • No subscription, renewal or upgrade fee
  • No invoice or voucher limits
  • No user seats, no per-company charge
  • No telemetry, no analytics SDK, no ad tracking
  • Works with the network cable unplugged

What it actually does

It is a double-entry engine, not a spreadsheet with a form on top. Every voucher must balance before it posts. Amounts are held as exact decimals and integer minor units rather than IEEE floats, so a balance sheet totals to zero instead of to 0.00000000001.

Day-to-day it covers the workflow an Indian trader or a small professional firm runs: ledgers and groups, sales and purchase vouchers, payment, receipt, contra and journal entries, GST with automatic CGST/SGST for intra-state parties and IGST for inter-state, stock items with weighted-average cost, a Day Book, a two-sided Balance Sheet and a two-sided Profit & Loss.

Where it deliberately stays small

It does not do payroll, it does not file your returns with a tax portal, and it does not have a mobile app store listing. If your requirement is a 200-seat multi-entity consolidation with approval workflows, this is the wrong tool and a paid ERP is the honest answer.

It is aimed at the business that needs correct books, GST-compliant invoices, and a file it can hand to its auditor — without renting that capability forever.

How to evaluate it in ten minutes

Download the Windows installer or the portable ZIP, create a company, and post one sales voucher with GST. Then open the Balance Sheet. If the two sides match and the tax split is correct for your state, you have verified the core of the product. The balanced figure is the whole point.

Questions

Is it really free forever, or free until I outgrow it?

MIT licence, no feature gates. There is no paid tier to graduate into, so there is nothing to outgrow.

Does free mean my data is the product?

No. The backend binds to 127.0.0.1 and never calls out. There is no telemetry, and the only network traffic the app generates is your own browser talking to your own machine.

Do I need to be online to use it?

No. The only time you need a connection is the download itself.

Can I use it for client bookkeeping as an accountant?

Yes. Nothing in the licence restricts commercial use, and you can create as many companies as you need.
